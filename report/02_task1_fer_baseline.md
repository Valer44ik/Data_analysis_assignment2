```latex
\section{Task 1: FER2013 baseline}
\subsection{Method}
\textbf{Features.} (i) \emph{HOG}: 9 orientations, $8\times8$-pixel cells, $2\times2$-cell blocks
with L2-Hys normalisation (900 dimensions); (ii) \emph{landmarks}: the 68 dlib points predicted on
the upscaled ($96\times96$) face, centred on their mean and scaled per axis as in the provided
baseline (136 dimensions). FER images are tight crops, so the whole image is the face box; for
crops made with a margin $m$ (KDEF, webcam) the predictor receives the inner box, the central
$1/(1+2m)$ of the crop --- giving it the whole KDEF crop misplaced the points (KDEF geometric
features: 0.28 vs 0.67 macro-F1 with a logistic regression); (iii) \emph{PCA}: raw pixels projected on the first eigenfaces
(whitened; 60 or 120 components chosen on validation); (iv) \emph{HOG+LM}: concatenation.
\textbf{Classifiers.} Logistic regression (LR), SVM with RBF kernel, random forest (RF, 300 trees),
each preceded by standardisation. \textbf{Protocol.} Hyper-parameters (regularisation $C$, class
weighting, tree depth/leaf size, PCA size) are chosen by grid search with a predefined split ---
fit on (a stratified 10k subset of) Training, macro-F1 on PublicTest --- then the model is refitted
on the full Training set. PrivateTest is used only for the numbers reported here. We optimise
macro-F1 rather than accuracy because a classifier that ignores Disgust loses only 1.5\,\% accuracy.
The provided RF+landmarks baseline reaches \todo{acc} accuracy / \todo{F1} macro-F1 on PrivateTest
in our pipeline (kit: 0.44 / 0.38 on validation).

\textbf{Best classifier.} The \todo{SVM} is the best classifier for every feature type
(Table~\ref{tab:t1grid}); with HOG+LM it reaches \todo{acc}/\todo{bal-acc}/\todo{F1}. The RBF kernel
separates classes that are not linearly separable in HOG space (LR is \todo{x} F1 points lower),
while the random forest's random feature subsets mostly see individually weak, highly correlated
HOG cells and it collapses towards the majority classes (lowest balanced accuracy).
\textbf{Best features.} HOG is the strongest single representation (\todo{}); it encodes the local
gradient structure of the mouth, eyes, brows and wrinkles and is invariant to global brightness.
Landmarks alone are weaker on FER (\todo{}) because dlib's shape predictor is unreliable on
$48\times48$, non-frontal, partially occluded faces (Fig.~\ref{fig:landmarks} in the appendix), but they add
complementary geometry: HOG+LM is best overall (\todo{}). PCA on pixels is the weakest: the
leading eigenfaces capture illumination, pose and identity rather than expression.

\textbf{Gray levels vs black-and-white.} Table~\ref{tab:t1pre} re-trains the best classifier on
five input variants. Binarising the face (Otsu or adaptive threshold) removes the grey-level
shading that carries the expression (nasolabial folds, brow furrows, mouth opening shadows) and
makes the result depend on illumination; it lowers macro-F1 by \todo{} points. Edge maps retain
the contours but lose texture (\todo{}). CLAHE (local contrast equalisation) is \todo{neutral/beneficial},
consistent with the lighting analysis below.

\subsection{Error analysis}
\textbf{Hardest emotions.} Per-class F1 (Fig.~\ref{fig:t1perclass}): Happy (\todo{}) and Surprise
(\todo{}) are easy --- they produce large, consistent geometric changes (smile, open mouth, raised
brows). The hardest are \todo{Fear (\,), Sad (\,) and Disgust (\,)}. The most frequent confusions
(\texttt{results/task1\_top\_confusions.csv}) are \todo{Fear$\to$Sad, Sad$\to$Neutral,
Angry$\to$Sad, Fear$\to$Surprise}: these pairs share action units (e.g.\ raised inner brows in
fear and sadness), and Disgust is mostly predicted as Angry because it has 16$\times$ fewer
examples and similar brow lowering.
\textbf{Lighting.} Accuracy drops from \todo{} on well-exposed, high-contrast images to \todo{} on
the darkest/lowest-contrast quintile (Fig.~\ref{fig:t1light}).
\textbf{Label noise.} The most confident errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:t1conf}) are in large part
mislabelled or ambiguous images, cartoons and non-faces; FER2013 also contains 59 groups of
identical training images with different labels. \textbf{Duplicates.} The 288 PrivateTest images
that also occur in Training are classified with \todo{} accuracy vs \todo{} for the others ---
a small optimistic bias of FER2013 test scores.
```
